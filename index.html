Hello reader,

Would you get into a taxi with no one behind the wheel? Robotaxis already carry paying passengers in a few cities, and for most people the first ride is the hardest one. There is no driver to confirm the address, help with a bag or explain a sudden stop.

Velo is my personal project. I wanted to design an iOS app that gives first-time riders enough confidence to book a self-driving car and stay calm during the ride, including people who use a wheelchair and people who are blind. In this case study I will show you how I got there.

01 · The problem

The car drives itself. The rider is on their own.

A driver does more than drive. While mapping a robotaxi trip from booking to drop-off, I kept coming back to four moments where the missing driver is felt the most:

01
No one to ask

Every answer a driver used to give now has to come from the app.

02
Which car is mine?

Without a driver waving, finding the right car at a busy curb is a guess.

03
Why did it do that?

A sudden stop or a slow turn feels scary when nobody explains it.

04
What if something goes wrong?

Riders need a way out: a safe stop, a real person, or the end of the ride.

Two groups lose the most when the driver disappears. Wheelchair users relied on the driver for the ramp and the straps, and blind riders relied on the driver to find them at the curb.

02 · Research

Learning what riders are offered today

This is a personal project, so I did not run interviews. Instead I studied how self-driving rides work today and what similar apps give their riders. I looked at three kinds of products:

01 · Robotaxi apps

Book, unlock and ride. Most of them assume the rider already trusts the car.

02 · Ride-hailing apps

Fast booking and a clear pickup, but help during the ride still comes from the driver.

03 · iOS accessibility

VoiceOver and Voice Control show what blind and low-vision users already expect from any app.

From this I wrote three goals that every screen had to pass:

Confident at every step

The app explains what is happening before the rider has to ask.

Help is one tap away

A real person, a safe stop and a way out on every ride screen.

Works for every rider

Wheelchair users and blind riders get the same flow, not a separate app.

03 · Who it is for

Meet Ana

Velo is made for people who use ride-hailing often but have never ridden without a driver. They are curious, a little nervous, and they want to know what the car is doing. I gathered these traits into one persona.

09 · Final thoughts

What I learned

The hardest part was knowing when the app should stay quiet. My first version had the car announce every turn. It felt safe on paper and tiring in use. Velo now speaks up only when something changes, and the rest of the time the ride screen simply says All normal.

I also learned that safety needs friction in the right places. Booking a ride should take seconds, but stopping a car in traffic should take a deliberate two-second hold.

Accessibility worked best as part of the main flow. A wheelchair or voice preference set once in onboarding changes every ride after it, instead of waiting in the settings for someone to find it.
